The beta distribution is

\[\mbox{Beta}(p | \alpha, \beta)=
\frac{\Gamma(\alpha) \Gamma(\beta)}{\Gamma(\alpha+\beta)}
p^{(\alpha-1)}(1-p)^{(\beta-1)} \]

where $\Gamma(z)=\int_0^{+\infty} t^{z-1}\exp(-t) dt$

If $p \sim \mbox{Beta}(p | \alpha, \beta)$ then

\begin{eqnarray*}
E[p] & = \mu =  & \frac{\alpha}{\alpha+\beta} \\
\mbox{Var}[p] & = \sigma^2 & = \frac{\alpha\beta}{(\alpha + \beta)^2(\alpha + \beta +1)} 
\end{eqnarray*}

From the equation for the variance one has
$\sigma^2 = \frac{\mu(1-\mu)}{(\alpha+\beta+1)}$ and therefore
$\alpha = \frac{\mu(\mu(1-\mu)-\sigma^2)}{\sigma^2}$ and
$\beta=\alpha\frac{1-\mu}{\mu}$.

Hence Beta can be expressed in terms $\alpha,\beta$ or equivalently  in terms
of its mean and variance. Since $\alpha > 0$, one has
$\mu(1-\mu)  - \sigma^2 > 0$ /i.e./ $\sigma^2 < \mu(1-\mu)$.
